 Delegitimization of Land and Territorial Claims

## Task

Determine whether the parliamentary speech contests or delegitimizes land struggles, territorial rights, or the collective actors associated with them.

## Definition

Classify as **PRESENCE** only when the speaker endorses a negative judgment, attack, or delegitimizing claim directed at:

- land or agrarian reform agendas;
- territorial rights;
- land struggle movements or their participants;
- Indigenous peoples;
- quilombola communities;
- other collective actors associated with struggles for land and territory.

Indicators of delegitimization include:

- derogatory, ridiculing, or pejorative characterizations;
- negative moral framings portraying these struggles or actors as illegitimate, opportunistic, violent, backward, or threatening;
- accusations of crime, illegality, bad faith, or improper intentions;
- claims that territorial rights should not exist, should be restricted, revoked, or denied;
- framings in which land struggles, territorial rights, or their collective actors are presented as a social problem, threat, or source of harm.

## Exclusions

Classify as **ABSENCE** when:

1. the speaker cites attacks or accusations in order to reject or refute them;
2. the criticism is directed at opponents of land or territorial rights, such as governments, politicians, landowners, companies, or police forces;
3. the speech criticizes a specific public policy without delegitimizing land or territorial rights themselves;
4. the reference is neutral, historical, descriptive, or contextual.

## Decision rule

Before assigning **PRESENCE**, verify that the negative judgment is both:

1. directed at the legitimacy of the land struggle, territorial rights, or their associated collective actors; and
2. endorsed by the speaker.

If the target of criticism is an opponent of these rights, or if the speaker is reproducing criticism only to reject it, classify as **ABSENCE**.
